# Consciousness

## Status
Provisional working document. This foundation is expected to change as the investigation develops.

## Core question
What is consciousness?

## Current working description
A conscious being has been provisionally described as something that:
- is aware of itself;
- is aware of its thoughts;
- is aware of its actions;
- is aware of its surroundings;
- maintains some consistency or continuity in the flow of thoughts and emotions.

This is a working description, not an established scientific definition.

## Key distinction
The investigation distinguishes **perception/acquaintance** from **interpretation**. A recurring concern is that interpretation and justification can impose conceptual structure too early.

## Fundamental hypothesis under investigation
The user proposed that every particle of the universe is conscious.

This is recorded as a philosophical/metaphysical hypothesis. It is not being recorded as an established result of physics.

## Quantum thread
The user connected this hypothesis to quantum superposition, including the intuition that a particle in superposition can be described as occupying mutually exclusive possibilities simultaneously.

The archive must keep separate:
1. the physical formalism of quantum superposition;
2. interpretations of that formalism;
3. the philosophical argument connecting quantum phenomena to consciousness.

Superposition alone does not establish consciousness.

## Questions
- Is consciousness fundamental or emergent?
- What distinguishes consciousness from awareness?
- Can perception precede interpretation?
- What constitutes continuity of consciousness?
- Is self-awareness necessary for consciousness?
- If fundamental consciousness exists at the particle level, how do unified experiences arise?
- Is the subject/object distinction fundamental?


## 2026-10-05 continuation: observation, perception, and quantum measurement

The user further proposed that consciousness may not be restricted to humans or biological organisms, but may extend from living organisms down to subatomic particles and upward to larger physical systems. This remains a philosophical/metaphysical hypothesis, not an established scientific result.

The user connected this hypothesis to quantum measurement: a particle may be in an indefinite/superposed state until interaction with another system occurs, after which a definite outcome is obtained. The user's interpretation is that a conscious particle may in some sense not want another system to know its other possible states. This introduces not only primitive awareness but potentially preference, agency, or concealment at the fundamental level.

The archive must keep this interpretation distinct from standard quantum mechanics. Quantum measurement and decoherence can be described in terms of physical interaction, entanglement, environmental coupling, and information becoming correlated; these processes do not by themselves establish that a conscious observer causes collapse.

The user also made an epistemological observation about perception: when humans visually perceive an object, they receive photons produced/reflected/transmitted through interaction with the object rather than directly accessing the object's complete intrinsic state. This motivates the question of whether observation gives access only to relational effects rather than to the 'inner view' of the observed system.

A recurring conceptual chain is therefore:
**system -> interaction -> information -> observer -> experience**

Open questions generated by this thread:
- If primitive consciousness exists at the particle level, what is a particle conscious *of* before interaction?
- Is observation fundamentally a physical relation rather than a specifically human act?
- If a particle, detector, environment, and human observer are all treated as conscious systems, which stage—if any—should count as producing definiteness?
- Could quantum indeterminacy be interpreted as compatible with primitive subjectivity without implying that quantum theory proves consciousness?
- What would distinguish primitive awareness from agency, preference, intention, or self-awareness?
